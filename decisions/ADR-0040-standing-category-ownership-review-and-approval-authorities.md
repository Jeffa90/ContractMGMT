# ADR-0040: A review before access is given, standing "all current and future categories" ownership, and approval authorities by name

Status: Implemented on the guided-administration branch (UX-9). Proposed until the owner reviews it; production approval remains external governance and is not declared.
Builds on ADR-0039 (access profiles as a presentation layer) and the confirmed decision that a category owner reads all and edits only the categories they own.

## Context

Running the owner's manual acceptance scenario against the first guided build found three gaps: there was no plain-English review before the first administrator's access was saved; "Own all categories" copied ownership onto the categories that existed that day, so every category added later had to be claimed by hand; and giving a colleague approval authority needed someone to invent and type an approver-group key, while a fresh organisation could not publish its set-up until approval rules had been authored although its workflow names a single approver group and needs no rules.

## Decision

1. **A review before anything is given.** Both the set-up step "Administrator access" and the People and Access wizard end in a Review access panel and an explicit confirmation. The panel says what the person will be able to do (with where), what they will not (formal review, approving their own submissions, records above their classification ceiling), and the organisation, profile, ownership, ceiling, approval memberships and administration access. It is built from the effective-access report of a **dry run of the real services that is rolled back** (the same technique as ADR-0039), so it cannot say more or less than saving would do. Role and permission codes are shown only in "Advanced details", which the administrator must ask for; at set-up there is no advanced view. Nothing is applied without the confirmation: the set-up stage and the wizard's save both refuse an unconfirmed request on the server.
2. **Standing ownership is a policy, not a copy.** "Own all current and future categories in this organisation" is stored as one small additive record, `CategoryOwnershipPolicy` (organisation, person, primary or backup, who set it, when, the profile application that created it, and the ids of the ownership assignments it made). **Category authorisation is unchanged**: the evaluator still reads ordinary ownership assignments. The policy keeps them in step: setting it covers the categories that exist; every category created afterwards, by any route (the Categories screen, set-up and onboarding, a data migration), is covered **in the same transaction as the category** through a small observer interface in the Contracts module (`ICategoryLifecycleObserver`) that the Access module implements. The observer uses the same trusted ownership service as set-up, so it adds no new authority. The policy is per organisation, so another organisation's categories are never covered; the table has row-level security like every tenant table.
3. **Existing ownership is respected.** A person who already owns a category (directly or through its parent) is left exactly as they are and is not claimed by the policy. Where another primary owner exists the policy owner becomes a backup; it never displaces anyone. Ending the policy (by ending the profile application that created it, ending Test-mode access, or ending the policy on the ownership screen) ends **only the assignments the policy made**.
4. **Effective access explains the result.** "Every current and future category of the organisation" appears in the person's ability text and in the reason ("a standing instruction, so a new one is theirs automatically"). Audit actions: `access.ownership_policy.set`, `.confirmed`, `.applied` (per new category) and `.ended`, alongside the existing `category.ownership_assigned` and `category.ownership_ended` for every assignment.
5. **Approval authorities by name.** A new page, Approval authorities, lists the authorities the organisation's workflows already name (from its published workflow, or from its pack when nothing is published yet) and any registered group, with the display name from the pack's terminology; the administrator picks members and backups by name and confirms a plain-English review. A new authority is registered under a key the platform generates from its name; the machine key is visible only under Advanced. Membership changes go through the existing approver-group service, so its delegation rules, empty-group protection and audit apply unchanged. Nothing about any pack is special-cased in platform code: the authority comes from the workflow definition the pack ships.
6. **Readiness no longer demands approval rules the workflow does not use.** Without a named matrix the single approver group decides (the reference model). The checklist now asks for approval rules only when the organisation's workflow names a matrix, and counts the selected pack's standard workflow as in place when nothing has been published yet.
7. **A completion panel.** When every Required item is done and the set-up is published, the checklist and the review page lead with "<Organisation> is ready to use" and four actions: create the first record, add another person, review the organisation set-up, go to Home.

## Alternatives considered

* **A virtual owner in the authorisation path** (the evaluator asking the policy as well as the assignments). Rejected: it changes how every category check is made and must be kept in step with each place ownership is read. Keeping ordinary assignments and a policy that maintains them leaves the security engine as it was.
* **Copying ownership at set-up only.** Rejected: that is the defect.
* **Registering the pack's approver group during set-up.** Not possible without weakening the rule that only a person who manages people may register a group; the group is registered when the administrator first chooses its people. Set-up grants the approval role against the pack's key, which the platform already accepts for an unregistered group.

## Consequences

* One additive migration (`Access: AddCategoryOwnershipPolicy`) joins the 1.1.0 upgrade manifest.
* The set-up wizard has one more step, "Administrator access", after notifications.
* A person who is invited and has never signed in now has an accurate effective-access report (previously empty), because the report loads the subject including invited people; the evaluator still refuses a subject that is not active for any real request.
