# ADR-0022: Approval matrix engine, plans, delegations and simulator

Status: Accepted (WFE-02, DR-2026-10-16)

## Context

Approvals are a platform capability, not JBS logic. JBS keeps its confirmed model: DocuSign handles Delegation of Authority, register approval is separate, and the Group Head of Procurement or a backup performs it (first response wins). Other solution packs need to configure value tiers, category and organisation conditions, several stages, parallel and all-must-agree steps, backups and delegation, and administrators must be able to see why a person was chosen before anything depends on it.

## Decision

1. **`Ecp.Approvals` (tier 6)** holds the matrix definition, the resolver, plan evaluation, delegations and the simulator. It references SharedKernel, Persistence, Audit, Tenancy, Identity and Configuration only. The Lifecycle module (tier 7) calls it; Approvals knows nothing about contracts or workflow states.
2. **The matrix is data.** `ApprovalMatrix` is a Configuration kind (draft, validate, publish, release history, rollback, audit). Documents are strict JSON (unknown members are errors) of matrices, each with rows. A row is Base or Exception (add steps or replace steps), has a priority, an optional effective window and conditions: value range (half-open `[min,max)`), category (with or without subcategories), contract type, legal entity, business unit, site, classification, risk level, supplier type and custom-field conditions using a closed operator list (`Equals`, `NotEquals`, `In`, `NotIn`, `AtLeast`, `LessThan`). There is no expression language, SQL, script or template.
3. **Validation is the safety net (V-005).** Publication is refused for overlapping value ranges, gaps, priority ties between rows that can match the same record, bad currency or rates, unknown vocabulary, and for removing a matrix a workflow still names. Open-ended ranges, unknown references and groups nobody holds are warnings. Ranges are compared per effective-date slice and only among rows that share every other condition.
4. **Resolution fails closed.** No matching row, a run-time tie, no rate for a currency difference, or a step nobody can perform gives a *blocked* result with a trace and a message; nothing is defaulted and unknown facts never satisfy a condition. There is no implicit currency conversion: a matrix has dated rate entries or it blocks.
5. **People come from groups through the access layer.** Group members are evaluated with the same `AccessEvaluator` as everything else (permission `approvals.action`, the record's organisation, scope dimensions and classification), so the submitter, disabled or expired people, people outside the scope, below the classification ceiling or privileged without a grant are excluded, each with a reason. Backups (Backup Approver role or a backup group) count only where the step allows. A delegate counts only for the seat of someone who is themselves eligible.
6. **Four response models.** Sequential rows open one stage per step; a parallel row opens all its steps together. A step is *First response wins* (one approval from anyone eligible) or *All responses required* (every named primary, optionally covered by a delegate). Any rejection on an open step rejects the whole plan. Plan evaluation is a pure function over the stored plan and the recorded responses.
7. **Plans are pinned.** At submission the resolver's plan, trace and explanations are stored with the approval request (`PlanJson`) together with the release number and a hash of the matrix content. Later publications do not change an in-flight request. Who may respond is judged against the group and delegations as they are *now* (so a leaver cannot approve), but the steps and groups are those pinned.
8. **Delegation is narrow.** A person delegates only their own authority, to a member of the same approver group or its backup group, for at most 90 days, never to themselves, never into a loop (graph cycle detection among overlapping delegations; loop edges in stored data are ignored and give nobody authority), not transitively, and it is revocable by the delegator or by `approvals.delegate.administer`. Use shows as "on behalf of". Everything is audited.
9. **Opt-in for lifecycle.** A workflow names a matrix with `settings.approval.matrix`. Without it the reference model runs exactly as before (`The_reference_model_is_unchanged_when_no_matrix_is_named`), and a matrix that models the same choice names exactly the same people (`A_matrix_that_models_the_reference_choice_names_exactly_the_same_people`). Intermediate approvals are recorded in `ApprovalStepResponse` (unique per step and person); the final approval and any rejection go through the same engine transition as the reference model, so Registered Date, snapshot, lock and notifications are unchanged. Two people answering at once produce exactly one decision (tested on SQL Server).
10. **Simulator and explanation.** The simulator uses the same resolver as submission (`The_simulator_and_a_real_submission_resolve_to_the_same_steps_and_people`). It can use the in-force matrix, a numbered release or the open draft, and any date (past or future). It needs `approvals.simulate`, stores nothing but an audit entry and reveals approver identities only to people who may see them. Each person is listed as included or excluded with the reasons; the trace lists every stage of selection.
11. **Permissions** added in the data catalogue: `approvals.matrix.read`, `approvals.simulate`, `approvals.delegate`, `approvals.delegate.administer`, awaiting owner confirmation.

## Consequences

* A future pack can configure a different approval model with a document and no code. Editing is through the configuration pages (draft, validate, publish); the matrix pages are read-only views, a simulator, delegations and history.
* Facts the platform does not hold (contract type, risk level, supplier type, legal entity, custom fields on a contract) reach the resolver only through an `IApprovalFactContributor` or the simulator; a condition on a fact nobody supplies never matches, so such a row blocks rather than approving wrongly.
* Residual and Future: timeouts and `escalateTo` are data only (no timers or messages); segregation of duties beyond the submitter is not configurable; a person responds once per step; an All-required step stalls if a named primary leaves (re-resolving an in-flight plan is Future); a visual matrix designer is STU-06.
